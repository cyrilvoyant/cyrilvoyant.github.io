Forecasting the intermittent solar resource: a course

Principle 2 · The model at the coreThe approach

In the story: understand the solar signal first, then climb the ladder of models one rung at a time, and only as far as it pays.

Course UE14, Mines Paris – PSL, 2026 · taught in French · slides and Python/Matlab practicals

How do you forecast solar irradiance one hour ahead, and how do you know whether your forecast is any good? This course answers both questions from scratch, on ten years of real hourly measurements from Ajaccio (Corsica, 1998–2007), and ends with the methods used in current research: clear-sky index, forecastability, NICE metrics, Extreme Learning Machines, online learning and frugal AI.

In English: Solar forecasting basics: a worked example on ten years of Ajaccio data, the whole course step by step with the real numbers.

Questions answered in the course

How do you forecast solar irradiance one hour ahead?

Start by removing the deterministic solar cycle with a clear-sky model: the course uses the clear-sky index as a difference, CSI = GHI − GHIcs (a ratio GHI / GHIcs is the other common choice), then centres it. Fit an autoregressive model AR(p) on it by ordinary least squares, choosing the order p on data not used for testing (ACF, PACF, AIC/BIC). Compare it with persistence and smart persistence, and add a nonlinear model such as an Extreme Learning Machine only if it beats them. The methods are reviewed in Voyant et al., Renewable Energy 2017 (doi:10.1016/j.renene.2016.12.095) and benchmarked in Voyant et al., Renewable Energy 2022 (doi:10.1016/j.renene.2022.04.065).

What is the clear-sky index in solar forecasting?

The gap between measured global horizontal irradiance and the irradiance a clear-sky model predicts for the same place and time, taken either as a difference (CSI = GHI − GHIcs, used in the course: about 0 under clear sky, negative under clouds) or as a ratio (GHI / GHIcs). It removes most of the daily and annual cycle, so that statistical models learn the cloud-driven part. The choice of clear-sky model matters for short-term forecasts (Voyant et al., Solar Energy 2025, doi:10.1016/j.solener.2025.113490).

How do you know whether a solar forecast is good?

Compare it with reference forecasts on the same samples: persistence and smart (clear-sky) persistence at least. Report normalized bias (nMBE) and error (nRMSE), a skill score against the reference, or the persistence-normalized NICEᵏ metrics, and say whether night hours are included.

How variable and forecastable is solar irradiance at a site?

The course measures site variability with the stochastic coefficient of variation (sCV) and a forecastability index F, introduced in Voyant et al., Renewable Energy 2026 (doi:10.1016/j.renene.2025.123913). They help compare sites before comparing forecasting models.

Course outline

  1. Why forecast solar power? Grid integration, irradiance vs irradiation vs PV output, the Ajaccio dataset.
  2. Solar radiation: direct and diffuse components, attenuation in the atmosphere, sun position, the double (daily and annual) seasonality of GHI.
  3. Time series and forecasting: components and decomposition, horizons and time steps, predictable part vs error, a worked AR(1) example.
  4. Clear sky and clear-sky index: the Solis clear-sky model, two ways to stationarize (difference or ratio), is the CSI stationary?, site variability with the stochastic coefficient of variation (sCV) and forecastability F, step-by-step preprocessing.
  5. Autoregressive models and least squares: from AR(p) to linear regression, building X and y by hand, ordinary least squares and its geometric meaning, train/validation/test split, choosing p with ACF, PACF, filter or wrapper approaches, AIC/BIC.
  6. Evaluation, ELM and online learning: reference models (persistence, smart persistence), nMBE, nRMSE, skill score and NICEΣ, day-only vs day-and-night scores, Extreme Learning Machines (principle, neurons, random draws, sin/cos time encoding), AR vs ELM, sliding-window online learning, frugality.

What students take away

  • A forecast is only useful if it beats a reference: always report persistence and smart persistence.
  • Remove or encode the deterministic solar cycle before learning, and state how (difference, ratio, or time encoding).
  • Choose model complexity from data you have not tested on; report the protocol (day only or day and night).
  • A smaller model that is refreshed often can rival a larger one: frugality is a design choice.
