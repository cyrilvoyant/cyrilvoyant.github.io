Temporal γ-index: tolerance-based validation of irradiance forecasts

Principle 5 · Trust is demonstratedThe approach

In the story: measure what a model adds, with tolerances that match the use, a method carried over from radiotherapy dose verification.

In brief

  • The temporal γ-index accepts a forecast point when an observation lies within joint tolerances in space (DTA, km), time (TTA, minutes) and intensity (IDT, W/m²); γ ≤ 1 passes.
  • It avoids the double penalty that pointwise scores such as RMSE give to a correct but slightly displaced cloud structure; the gamma pass rate (GPR) summarizes a whole field.
  • Published by C. Voyant in the International Journal of Forecasting 42(4), 1207-1214 (2026); MATLAB code, doi:10.5281/zenodo.21755936.

What does this forecast study add?

The γ-index for forecast verification, proposed by Cyril Voyant in the International Journal of Forecasting (2026, doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2026.03.002), accepts a forecast point when an observation agrees within joint spatial (DTA), temporal (TTA) and intensity tolerances, so displaced but correct cloud structures are not double-penalized.

Questions and answers

How can forecast verification account for spatial and temporal displacement?

Use a tolerance-based score instead of a pointwise one. The γ-index (Voyant, International Journal of Forecasting, 2026, doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2026.03.002) accepts a forecast point when an observation agrees within a spatial distance (DTA), a time shift (TTA) and an intensity difference (IDT). A cloud structure that is right but slightly displaced is then not penalized twice, as it is by RMSE or MAE.

Which tolerances are needed for gamma-index forecast evaluation?

Three: DTA, the spatial tolerance in kilometres; TTA, the temporal tolerance in minutes; and IDT, the intensity tolerance in W/m² for irradiance. Gamma ≤ 1 marks agreement within the combined criterion, and the Gamma Pass Rate is the share of points meeting it. Choose the tolerances from the application before comparing methods, report their sensitivity, and keep pointwise errors alongside gamma: looser tolerances always raise the pass rate.

Research question

Pointwise errors can penalize a forecast structure that is displaced in time or space. The gamma-index asks whether a forecast value agrees with a nearby observation within explicitly chosen spatial, temporal and intensity tolerances.

The theory

A pointwise score compares each forecast pixel with the observation at the same place and time, so a correct cloud forecast that is slightly displaced is penalised twice: once where the cloud was forecast, once where it occurred. The γ-index, carried over from radiotherapy dose verification (Low et al., 1998), instead asks whether an observation close enough in space, time and intensity agrees with the forecast.

Definitions

DTA
distance to agreement: spatial tolerance, in km
TTA
time to agreement: temporal tolerance, in minutes
IDT
intensity deviation tolerance, in W/m² for irradiance
\(\mathcal N\)
search neighbourhood, \(\tfrac32\)DTA in space and \(\tfrac32\)TTA in time, so that a match is always found if it exists

Equations

For a forecast at grid point \((x,y)\) and time \(t\), with \((x-x')^2+(y-y')^2\) in km² and \(t-t'\) in minutes:

$$\Gamma(x,y,t)=\min_{(x',y',t')\in\mathcal N}\sqrt{\frac{(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2}{\mathrm{DTA}^2}+\frac{(t-t')^2}{\mathrm{TTA}^2}+\frac{\big(\mathrm{pred}(x,y,t)-\mathrm{obs}(x',y',t')\big)^2}{\mathrm{IDT}^2}}$$

Each term is a distance divided by its tolerance, so each tolerance counts as 1. A point passes when \(\Gamma\le1\): some observation lies inside the ellipsoid of tolerances. Over the \(N\) evaluated points:

$$\mathrm{GPR}=\frac{100}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb 1\!\left(\Gamma_i\le1\right)\ \%,$$ $$\bar\Gamma=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Gamma_i,$$ $$\Gamma_{\max}=\max_i\Gamma_i .$$

GPR (gamma pass rate) is the share of the field within tolerance; \(\bar\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma_{\max}\) say how far the failing points are from the boundary.

Assumptions and validity

The tolerances are application choices, fixed before comparing methods; looser tolerances always raise GPR, so report them and their sensitivity, and keep pointwise errors (RMSE) alongside. The forecast and observed fields must share the grid and the time step after resampling. The method does not claim the invention of the medical-physics γ-index; it applies it to forecast verification.

Units in the reference implementation

computeGammaMetrics.m measures the spatial distance in grid cells and the time shift in image steps, then divides them by DTA (km) and TTA (minutes). The terms of the formula are in km and minutes only when one cell is 1 km and one step is 1 minute. In practice:

  • Space: give the forecast and observed maps on a 1 km grid and keep res_ini = res_end = 1. The built-in spatial rescaling multiplies the image size by res_end/res_ini, which coarsens the grid instead of refining it when res_end is smaller than res_ini.
  • Time: set timestep_end = 1/60 (hour). The function then interpolates the observed sequence linearly to 60 times as many images over the same time span, a step of about one minute (0.96 min for 24 hourly maps), which multiplies the run time.
  • Otherwise, convert the distances to km and minutes before comparing them with the tolerances.

The public example uses 1 km cells (consistent) and 1-hour steps with timestep_end = 1: its temporal term is in hours divided by minutes, so a one-hour shift weighs \((1/30)^2\) instead of \((60/30)^2\).

Tolerances are part of the result

Choose tolerances from the application before comparing methods and report sensitivity. Looser tolerances increase acceptance; a high pass rate alone does not prove a useful forecast. The repository's example thresholds are not universal validation standards. Report pointwise errors alongside gamma and document edge effects and resampling.
