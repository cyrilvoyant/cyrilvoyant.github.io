The γ-index: tolerance-based validation of spatio-temporal forecast fields

Principle 5 · Trust is demonstratedThe approach

In the story: measure what a model adds, with tolerances that match the use, a method carried over from radiotherapy dose verification.

In brief

  • The 3D γ-index accepts a forecast point when an observation lies within joint tolerances in space (DTA), time (TTA) and intensity (IDT): γ ≤ 1 passes, and the γ passing rate (GPR) and the mean γ summarize a field.
  • It addresses the double penalty that pixel-wise scores such as RMSE give to small displacements of coherent structures; over Corsica (DTA = 1 pixel ≈ 2 km, TTA = 30 min, IDT = 50 W/m²) the article proposes GPR > 80 % and a mean γ below 0.7 as practical thresholds.
  • Published by C. Voyant in the International Journal of Forecasting 42(4), 1207-1214 (2026); MATLAB code, doi:10.5281/zenodo.21755936.

What does this forecast study add?

This work applies joint spatial, temporal and intensity tolerances to forecast verification. Tolerances represent application choices; the work does not claim invention of the medical-physics gamma-index.

Cyril Voyant · Updated 2026-10-20

The γ-index for forecast verification, proposed by Cyril Voyant in the International Journal of Forecasting (2026, doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2026.03.002), accepts a forecast point when an observation agrees within joint spatial (DTA), temporal (TTA) and intensity tolerances, so displaced but correct cloud structures are not double-penalized.

Questions and answers

How can forecast verification account for spatial and temporal displacement?

Use a tolerance-based score instead of a pointwise one. The γ-index (Voyant, International Journal of Forecasting, 2026, doi:10.1016/j.ijforecast.2026.03.002) accepts a forecast point when an observation agrees within a spatial distance (DTA), a time shift (TTA) and an intensity difference (IDT). A cloud structure that is right but slightly displaced is then not penalized twice, as it is by RMSE or MAE.

Which tolerances are needed for gamma-index forecast evaluation?

Three: DTA, the spatial tolerance in kilometres; TTA, the temporal tolerance in minutes; and IDT, the intensity tolerance in W/m² for irradiance. Gamma ≤ 1 marks agreement within the combined criterion, and the Gamma Pass Rate is the share of points meeting it. The article sets DTA to one grid spacing, TTA to half a time step when images are interpolated (30 min in its Corsica study) and IDT to an acceptable deviation (50 W/m², about 10 %); a ±50 % change of a tolerance changed GPR by less than 10 % in its tests. Keep pointwise errors and the bias alongside γ.

Research question

Pointwise errors can penalize a forecast structure that is displaced in time or space. The gamma-index asks whether a forecast value agrees with a nearby observation within explicitly chosen spatial, temporal and intensity tolerances.

The theory

Why, in the words of the article: local scores such as RMSE or MAE severely over-penalize small spatial or temporal displacements of coherent structures, the double penalty issue. A correct cloud forecast slightly displaced is penalized once where the cloud was forecast and once where it occurred. The 3D γ-index, introduced for radiotherapy dose verification (Low et al., 1998) and applied here for the first time to spatio-temporal forecast validation, maps forecast and observation into a normalized tolerance space where location, time and intensity form a single distance. The equation numbers are those of the article.

Definitions

DTA
distance to agreement: spatial tolerance
TTA
time to agreement: temporal tolerance
IDT
intensity deviation tolerance, for example 50 W/m², calibrated from sensor uncertainty or grid operational limits
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{DTA}},\ \Omega_{\mathrm{TTA}}\)
search neighborhoods \(|x-x'|\le\tfrac32\mathrm{DTA},\ |y-y'|\le\tfrac32\mathrm{DTA}\) and \(|t-t'|\le\tfrac32\mathrm{TTA}\), beyond the tolerance boundary so that a feasible match is identified

The γ-index (Eq. 1) and its passing rate (Eq. 2)

$$\gamma(x,y,t)=\min_{\substack{(x',y')\in\Omega_{\mathrm{DTA}}\\ t'\in\Omega_{\mathrm{TTA}}}}\sqrt{\frac{(x-x')^2+(y-y')^2}{\mathrm{DTA}^2}+\frac{(t-t')^2}{\mathrm{TTA}^2}+\frac{\big(I_{\mathrm{pred}}(x,y,t)-I_{\mathrm{obs}}(x',y',t')\big)^2}{\mathrm{IDT}^2}},$$ $$(1)$$

A prediction passes when \(\gamma(x,y,t)\le1\), the boundary of the unit tolerance region in the normalized γ space. With \(N=N_xN_yN_t\):

$$\mathrm{GPR}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb 1\!\left(\gamma_i\le1\right)\times100\,\%,$$ $$(2),$$ $$\bar\gamma=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_i,$$ $$\gamma_{\max}=\max_i\gamma_i .$$

The γ passing rate (GPR) is bounded; \(\gamma_{\max}\) highlights localized important mismatches that domain averages hide.

Choosing the tolerances (§2.3)

  • Keep them consistent with the native resolution: DTA is set to one grid spacing (one pixel); TTA can be limited to half a time step (\(\Delta t/2\)) when consecutive images are interpolated linearly; IDT is an acceptable relative deviation, often 10 % for radiative or energy quantities.
  • The article adopts DTA = 1 pixel (≈ 2 km), TTA = 30 min and IDT = 50 W/m² over Corsica. Sensitivity tests: a ±50 % change of DTA, TTA or IDT changes GPR by less than 10 % and \(\bar\gamma\) by less than 0.3, for these conditions.
  • Practical thresholds proposed in the conclusions: GPR > 80 % and \(\bar\gamma<0.7\) for a reliable forecast field.

What the article shows

On HelioClim-3 and CAMS fields over Corsica (about 2 km, a decade of data), with controlled displacements and noise, the pixel-wise RMSE varies between 20.95 and 37.65 W/m², while \(\bar\gamma\) stays between 0.29 and 0.35 and GPR between 67.35 and 68.64 %: the γ criterion is robust to small displacements and isolates real discrepancies. The two scores agree under clear or fully overcast skies; they differ most under broken clouds, where sharp gradients amplify pointwise penalties. Keep the bias (MBE) alongside: it reveals monotonic shifts even when γ is acceptable.

Limitations (§5) and perspective

The temporal component is not fully exploited in this first implementation (the validation focuses on the spatial dimension); a finer TTA or a full temporal search is needed for transient phenomena, and a TTA equal to the whole sampling interval can favor persistence. DTA = 1 pixel suits moderate resolutions (1 to 5 km), not grids coarser than 5 to 10 km. The 10 % IDT is a baseline; the operational rules of each variable should set it. Because γ is continuous and differentiable almost everywhere, the article suggests, beyond its scope, a tolerance-aware training loss \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}+\lambda\,\mathbb E[\max(0,\gamma-1)]\) (Eq. 3).

Units in the reference implementation

computeGammaMetrics.m measures the spatial distance in grid cells and the time shift in image steps, then divides them by DTA (km) and TTA (minutes). The terms of the formula are in km and minutes only when one cell is 1 km and one step is 1 minute. In practice:

  • Space: give the forecast and observed maps on a 1 km grid and keep res_ini = res_end = 1. The built-in spatial rescaling multiplies the image size by res_end/res_ini, which coarsens the grid instead of refining it when res_end is smaller than res_ini.
  • Time: set timestep_end = 1/60 (hour). The function then interpolates the observed sequence linearly to 60 times as many images over the same time span, a step of about one minute (0.96 min for 24 hourly maps), which multiplies the run time.
  • Otherwise, convert the distances to km and minutes before comparing them with the tolerances.

The public example uses 1 km cells (consistent) and 1-hour steps with timestep_end = 1: its temporal term is in hours divided by minutes, so a one-hour shift weighs \((1/30)^2\) instead of \((60/30)^2\).

Tolerances are part of the result

Choose tolerances from the application before comparing methods and report sensitivity. The article proposes GPR > 80 % and a mean γ below 0.7 as practical thresholds; a pass rate alone does not replace the bias and pointwise errors. The repository's example thresholds are not universal validation standards. Report pointwise errors alongside gamma and document edge effects and resampling.
