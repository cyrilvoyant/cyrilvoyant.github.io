Make_Stationary: does deseasonalization improve solar and wind forecasts?

Principle 3 · Complexity only if it paysThe approach

In the story: climb only if it pays. Deseasonalization is kept only when it improves the forecast.

In brief

  • The benchmark tests whether removing the daily and annual cycle (six methods, including two ELM operators) improves the final forecast of nine Corsican energy and weather series, 1 to 6 hours ahead.
  • A more stationary residual is not a more predictable one: the gain depends on the signal (about 3 points of nRMSE for hourly wind speed), and raw forecasting stays strongest for the other variables.
  • M. Agoundedemba, C. Voyant, M. Despotović, L. A. Garcia-Gutierrez and G. Notton, manuscript submitted; MATLAB code and data, doi:10.5281/zenodo.21490743.

Does deseasonalization help solar and wind forecasting? The Make_Stationary benchmark

What does this benchmark examine?

The benchmark separates deseasonalization quality from downstream forecasting utility. A more stationary residual does not by itself establish a better forecast. The associated paper is submitted, not presented here as accepted.

Cyril Voyant · Updated 2026-10-20

Make_Stationary (M. Agoundedemba, C. Voyant, M. Despotović, L. A. Garcia-Gutierrez, G. Notton; software doi:10.5281/zenodo.21490744; companion manuscript submitted, 2026) tests whether deseasonalization improves the final solar and wind forecast, and shows that a more stationary residual is not necessarily a more predictable one.

Questions about deseasonalization and forecasting

Does deseasonalization always improve renewable energy forecasting?

No. Removing a recurring cycle can improve residual stationarity without improving the final forecast. Evaluate the full pipeline: fit the seasonal component on training data, forecast the residual, reconstruct the signal, and compare with forecasting the raw signal on identical test samples and horizons.

How can I test whether preprocessing helps?

Use a chronological training/test split, prevent information from future observations entering the seasonal estimate, and keep scoring windows and baseline definitions fixed. Report absolute forecast errors alongside a persistence-relative metric such as NICEᵏ. Test both the raw and preprocessed pipelines, rather than selecting a transform from a stationarity diagnostic alone.

These answers summarize the public benchmark protocol. The companion manuscript remains submitted; this guide does not turn its status into a published journal article.

Key finding

A cleaner residual is not a more predictable one. On nine energy and weather series from Corsica, forecast 1 to 6 hours ahead, removing the daily and annual cycle gives the most stationary residuals with the rolling median and the two ELM operators, but this ranking does not carry over to forecast accuracy.

  • Forecasting gains depend on the signal and the horizon: about 3 percentage points of nRMSE for hourly wind speed, neutral or mildly detrimental for PV power, total electrical load and wind power.
  • PHASE, the lighter of the two frugal ELM operators, gives the best overall compromise and ranks ahead of PROJECTION on average. Raw forecasting remains the strongest strategy for the non-wind variables.
  • Deseasonalization is therefore a selective preprocessing option, not a universal step.

The wind-speed improvement above is reported in the public repository README. The manuscript is submitted to Energy Reports. Numerical experiments were not re-run for this page.

Stationarity and forecast quality

A residual diagnostic and a forecast evaluation answer different questions. Compare reconstructed forecasts against RAW on the same samples and horizons; do not select preprocessing from stationarity diagnostics alone. Detailed numerical ranks from the submitted manuscript are not reproduced here.

The theory

Deseasonalization removes the recurring daily and annual cycle, forecasts what is left, and adds the cycle back. The question is whether the final forecast improves, not whether the residual looks more stationary.

Decomposition

$$Y_t=S_t+R_t,$$ $$\hat Y_{t+h}=\hat S_{t+h}+\hat R_{t+h}.$$

The split is additive: a ratio such as a clear-sky index would be singular at night for GHI and PV. The daily and annual phases are encoded by sine–cosine pairs,

$$\mathbf p_t=\left[\sin\tfrac{2\pi\,\tau_d(t)}{24},\ \cos\tfrac{2\pi\,\tau_d(t)}{24},\ \sin\tfrac{2\pi\,\tau_y(t)}{365},\ \cos\tfrac{2\pi\,\tau_y(t)}{365}\right],$$

with \(\tau_d\) the hour of the day and \(\tau_y\) the day of the year.

The two ELM operators

  • PROJECTION: an ELM (fixed random tanh hidden layer, ridge output weights) reads lagged values and \(\mathbf p_t\); at inference the lags are set to their training mean, so the output depends only on the phase: \(\hat S_t=f(\bar{\mathbf y},\mathbf p_t)\).
  • PHASE: the same ELM with \(\mathbf p_t\) as its only input: \(\hat S_t=f(\mathbf p_t)\).
$$\beta=\left(H^{\top}H+\lambda I\right)^{-1}H^{\top}Y,$$ $$\lambda=10^{-4}\ \text{in the code}.$$

Benchmarks of \(\hat S\): crossed diurnal × annual Fourier regression, LOESS and a ±10-day rolling-median template. Each residual, and the raw signal, is forecast by an ELM and by a linear AR model; all scores (nRMSE, nMAE, nMBE, R², NICE) are computed on \(\hat Y\) in physical units.

Assumptions and validity

\(S\) is fitted on training data only. One region, three years, horizons up to 6 hours. The repository documents three differences with the manuscript (fixed ridge, selection among 300 hidden layers with an affine recalibration of \(S\), mixed sigmoid and Gaussian forecasting neurons); see below.

Data

Nine variables over 2021–2023, all included in the repository (about 5 MB):

  • GHI and 2 m temperature at Ajaccio, at 30 min and 1 h;
  • 10 m wind speed at Bastia, at 30 min and 1 h (Météo-France);
  • island-wide PV power, wind power and total load, hourly (EDF Corsica open data).

Reproduce

MATLAB with the Econometrics and Signal Processing toolboxes. The Parallel Computing Toolbox is optional and only speeds up runs.

Optimization/optimization_all_variables.m        % 1. grid search of lags L and neurons m
Deseasonalization/main_deseasonalization.m       % 2. 6 methods x 9 variables -> *_FULL.csv, Metrics.csv
Forecasting/main_forecast_GHI_and_GHI_30min.m    % 3a. GHI, with clear-sky references
Forecasting/main_forecasting_PV_WT_PT_WS_T2M.m   % 3b. other variables

Run each stage from its own folder. Two different functions share the name Make_stationary_all_variables.m, and having both folders on the MATLAB path silently shadows one of them.

Paper vs code: documented differences

The repository states these openly. Read them before comparing your numbers with the manuscript.

  • The ridge parameter is fixed at λ = 10⁻⁴ in the code. Only (L, m) are grid-searched, whereas the paper describes a ridge search.
  • The final pipeline keeps the best of 300 random hidden layers using lag-24 residual autocorrelation, then applies an affine recalibration of S. The recalibration is not in the paper's equations.
  • The forecasting ELM uses mixed sigmoid and Gaussian neurons, a detail not written in the paper.
  • The plotting utility daviolinplot.m has no license header, and its license is not verified.

Scope

This is a benchmark protocol, not evidence that ELM deseasonalization is better in general. Results come from one region, one three-year period and horizons of at most 6 h. The general lesson is methodological: judge a preprocessing step by the accuracy of the final forecast against the raw signal, not by how stationary the residual looks.
