Radioiodine therapy in metastatic thyroid cancer: the RAIR-Sim model

Principle 2 · The model at the coreThe approach

In the story: model first, then hybridize. Three equations calibrated on 50 patients explore radioiodine protocols; learning will be added only where the model falls short. The work of Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with Cyril Voyant and David Taieb.

In brief

  • Three equations describe the activity of radioactive iodine, the tumour cells and thyroglobulin; calibrated on 50 patients, they compare radioiodine protocols before any treatment.
  • The tumour-cell doubling time separates responders (72.5 % of the cohort, 66.6 months) from refractory patients (27.5 %, 9.8 months), and the model indicates the status after two or three sessions instead of seven or eight.
  • M. Fusella Giuntini, C. Voyant, D. Taieb and D. Barbolosi, Scientific Reports 16, 27179 (2026); RAIR-Sim code by Marie Fusella Giuntini, documented fork doi:10.5281/zenodo.21496197.

Three equations describe how metastatic thyroid cancer responds to repeated sessions of radioactive iodine. Calibrated on 50 patients, they explore the number of sessions, the interval between them and the activity before any patient is treated; learning is planned only where the model falls short.

The model and its simulator, RAIR-Sim, are the work of Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with Cyril Voyant and David Taieb.

The question

Differentiated thyroid cancer that has spread is often treated with repeated sessions of radioactive iodine (RAI). Some tumours respond; others become refractory. The blood level of thyroglobulin (Tg) follows the tumour burden. How many sessions, how far apart, and at what activity, for which profile of patient? A model that can be read term by term lets these choices be compared in silico before treatment.

The theory

Model first. In the words of the article: marked inter-patient variability makes the choice of a radioiodine protocol difficult, between efficacy and radioiodine-induced toxicity. A validated mechanistic compartmental model of three differential equations links the protocol (number of sessions \(n\), interval between sessions, activity per session \(A\)) to the serum thyroglobulin, and the tumour-cell doubling time \(T_d\) separates responders from non-responders. Future developments, according to the article, will improve patient-specific parameter estimation, including supervised learning to update the parameters at each session.

Definitions

\(A(t)\)
activity of the radioactive iodine present (GBq)
\(N(t)\)
number of tumour cells
\(Tg(t)\)
thyroglobulin in the blood (ng/mL), the clinical biomarker
\(D,\ n,\ \Delta T\)
activity per session (GBq), number of sessions, interval between sessions (months)
\(T_d\)
tumour doubling time (months): what separates a refractory from a responding profile

Equations

$$\frac{dA}{dt}=-a\ln2\,A,$$ $$\frac{dN}{dt}=N\left(\frac{\ln2}{T_d}-r_0\,A\right),$$ $$\frac{dTg}{dt}=\lambda\,N-k_e\,Tg .$$

The activity decays at the effective rate \(a\ln2\) (radioactive and biological clearance together); the tumour grows at \(\ln2/T_d\) and is killed in proportion to cells and activity present; thyroglobulin is secreted by the tumour cells and cleared at rate \(k_e\).

Session by session

At session \(i\), a bolus \(D\) is added to the residual activity, \(A_i=D+A_{i-1}e^{-a\ln2\,\Delta T}\). Between sessions, with \(t\) counted from the session:

$$A(t)=A_i\,e^{-a\ln2\,t},$$ $$N(t)=N_i\exp\!\left(\frac{\ln2}{T_d}\,t-\frac{r_0A_i}{a\ln2}\left(1-e^{-a\ln2\,t}\right)\right),$$ $$Tg(t)=e^{-k_et}\left(Tg_i+\lambda\int_0^t N(s)\,e^{k_es}\,ds\right).$$

Each administration is an instantaneous input on the activity compartment; tumour burden and thyroglobulin stay continuous at injection times. Between two sessions the system is solved analytically in closed form, with constant parameters, not by time discretization: this is the mathematical basis of RAIR-Sim (the public code uses MATLAB dsolve).

What the article shows

  • Cohort: 50 patients treated at AP-HM (Marseille), anonymized retrospective data 2014–2016; parameters estimated with the MCMC-SAEM algorithm in Monolix.
  • Two profiles: the mixture algorithm separates responders (72.5 % of patients, mean \(T_d\) = 66.6 months), whose thyroglobulin decreases progressively, from non-responders or refractory patients (27.5 %, mean \(T_d\) = 9.8 months), whose thyroglobulin first decreases then rises again.
  • Earlier decision: the model indicates the responder or non-responder status after two or three sessions and three thyroglobulin measurements, instead of the conventional seven or eight sessions.
  • Protocols: for responders, the aim is the minimal effective activity and longer intervals, to limit toxicity; for refractory patients, the study explores the limits of the protocol (higher activities, more sessions, shorter intervals). RAIR-Sim simulates these trajectories from patient-specific inputs, with an initial thyroglobulin of 100 ng/mL in the article's simulations.

Assumptions and validity

Parameters calibrated on a cohort of 50 patients (MCMC-SAEM, Monolix) and shared by the two archetypes except \(T_d\); a deliberately simple description of residual activity and tumour response. A research tool, not a clinical decision tool; prospective validation is needed.

Run it

Python 3, NumPy and SciPy; executed on 23 October 2026. The values match the original MATLAB demonstrators to within 0.02 ng/mL (difference due to the sampling of fplot).

import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

# parameters of the public demonstrators (github.com/MarieFG49/CTD_SIMU_RAI)
r0, a, lam, ke = 0.00407, 0.0169, 3.86e-9, 0.319
N0, Tg0 = 1.12e9, 100.0
k = a * np.log(2)


def tg_at(t_end, Td, n_sessions, lag=3.0, dose=3.7):
    N, Tg, A_res, t0 = N0, Tg0, 0.0, 0.0
    for i in range(n_sessions):
        A0 = dose + A_res                     # new bolus on top of the residual activity
        t1 = (i + 1) * lag if i < n_sessions - 1 else t_end
        rhs = lambda t, y: [y[0] * (np.log(2) / Td - r0 * A0 * np.exp(-k * (t - t0))), lam * y[0] - ke * y[1]]
        N, Tg = solve_ivp(rhs, (t0, t1), [N, Tg], rtol=1e-10, atol=1e-6, method="LSODA").y[:, -1]
        A_res, t0 = A0 * np.exp(-k * lag), t1
    return Tg


for Td in (9.8, 66.6):
    print(f"Td = {Td:4} months: Tg(79) = {tg_at(79, Td, 1):8.2f} ng/mL after 1 session, {tg_at(79, Td, 8):6.2f} after 8")

Output:

Td =  9.8 months: Tg(79) =  1385.54 ng/mL after 1 session,  12.06 after 8
Td = 66.6 months: Tg(79) =    14.01 ng/mL after 1 session,   0.13 after 8

Results of the demonstrators

The public demonstrators share every parameter except the tumour doubling time: Td = 9.8 months for a refractory profile and 66.6 months for a responding one. With 3.7 GBq per session every 3 months:

ProfileTd (months)Tg at 79 months, 1 session (ng/mL)Tg at 79 months, 8 sessions (ng/mL)
Refractory9.81,385.512.1
Responding66.614.00.13

For the refractory profile, a single session only delays the regrowth: thyroglobulin climbs again after about a year. Eight sessions keep it low. For the responding profile, one session already brings thyroglobulin down; more sessions push it towards zero. These are model outputs for two archetypes, not predictions for a patient.

What the published study does

All parameters, including the doubling time itself, were calibrated on a cohort of 50 patients with the MCMC-SAEM algorithm (Monolix). The study then runs a sensitivity analysis of the three protocol parameters (number of sessions, interval between sessions and activity per session) on the thyroglobulin kinetics, and identifies the tumour doubling time as the quantity that separates responding from refractory profiles.

Towards a hybrid digital twin

The next step keeps the model at the core and adds learning only where it falls short: a hybrid digital twin, in which a frugal learner (an extreme learning machine, for example) estimates what the three equations do not capture, informed by medicine and mathematics rather than replacing them. This work is in progress and separate from the published results.

Limits

Two archetypes and population-calibrated parameters; residual activity and tumour response are described by a deliberately simple model. RAIR-Sim is a research tool: it is not a clinical decision tool and has not been validated prospectively.
