NICEᵏ metrics for solar forecast evaluation

Cyril Voyant · Updated 2026-10-20

The NICEᵏ metrics (Normalized Informed Comparison of Errors), introduced by Cyril Voyant and co-authors in Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments (2025, doi:10.1016/j.seta.2025.104588), score a deterministic solar forecast against persistence-based references, so that a value below 1 means the forecast beats the reference.

Les métriques NICEᵏ (Normalized Informed Comparison of Errors), proposées par Cyril Voyant et coll. (Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.seta.2025.104588), comparent une prévision solaire déterministe à des références de persistance : une valeur inférieure à 1 signifie que la prévision fait mieux que la référence.

Questions about NICEᵏ solar forecast metrics

What do NICE metrics measure in deterministic solar forecasting?

NICEᵏ compares a forecast error norm with the error norm of a specified persistence forecast on the same observations. It is a reference-relative measure of deterministic forecast accuracy. Here, NICEᵏ refers to the solar forecasting framework published by Voyant and coauthors in 2025, with DOI 10.1016/j.seta.2025.104588.

What does a NICE component below one mean in solar forecasting?

For the component defined as the ratio of L-k error norms, a value below one means smaller errors than the chosen persistence baseline. A value of 0.8 means that this error norm is 20% smaller on the evaluated samples. It does not imply a 20% improvement in every error metric or in economic value. A zero baseline norm makes the ratio undefined.

Que mesurent les métriques NICE pour la prévision solaire ?

Les métriques NICEᵏ comparent les erreurs d’une prévision déterministe à celles d’une persistance définie, sur les mêmes observations. Les composantes de différents ordres donnent un poids différent aux grandes erreurs. La définition du modèle de référence et de la fenêtre d’évaluation fait partie du résultat.

Que signifie un score NICE inférieur à un en prévision solaire ?

Pour une composante NICEᵏ définie comme rapport de normes d’erreur, une valeur inférieure à 1 signifie une erreur plus faible que celle de la persistance choisie. Il faut préciser la composante : un agrégat ou une transformation du score doit être interprété avec sa propre formule.

Why a reference matters

An absolute error describes forecast quality on a given series. A reference-relative error also asks whether a model improves over a specified baseline on exactly the same observations.

For forecast errors e and persistence errors p, the NICE component of order k is the ratio of their L-k norms:

NICE_k = (sum(abs(e)^k) / sum(abs(p)^k))^(1/k)

For the same number of samples this also equals the ratio of the corresponding mean-power errors. Orders 1, 2 and 3 place progressively more weight on large deviations.

Interpretation

  • Below 1: smaller error norm than the selected persistence baseline.
  • Equal to 1: the same norm as that baseline.
  • Above 1: worse than the baseline on that component.

Do not confuse the error ratio with a skill score defined as one minus the ratio. State the exact formula whenever an aggregated NICE quantity or transformed score is used.

Small reproducible example

With errors [1, 2] and persistence errors [2, 4], every component is 0.5. If the persistence error norm is zero, the ratio is undefined: report that case rather than silently adding an arbitrary constant.

Reporting protocol

State persistence definition, horizon, temporal alignment, scoring window and missing-value mask. Report absolute errors alongside the ratio. A ratio below one does not establish operational value or superiority to every possible baseline.
