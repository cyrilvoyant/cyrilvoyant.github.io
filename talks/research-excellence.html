Research excellence in 2026: beyond publish and pray

Talk (YouTube, 45 min, February 2026) and preprint (SSRN, 2026)

Publishing is no longer enough. Global scientific output has grown fourfold since 2004 and AI writing tools accelerate it further. In this context, research impact depends not only on quality but on whether the work can be found, trusted and adopted.

Watch the talk (45 min)

The RTVACOS framework

The companion preprint, Beyond publish and pray: engineering research excellence (C. Voyant, R. Amaro e Silva, N. Fichaux, L. Menard, S. Cros, R.-E. Mamouri), proposes a heuristic framework that treats research excellence as a multiplicative system:

  • Foundations: Rigour, Transparency, Visibility, Adoption. A weakness in any one collapses overall impact, whatever the performance elsewhere.
  • Amplifiers: Collaboration, Outreach, Societal relevance. They scale a solid foundation but cannot replace one.

It is a mirror for reflection, not a scoring instrument; the authors claim no empirical validation and discuss limits such as gamification risk and unequal skill distribution.

Questions about research excellence

What is the RTVACOS framework for research excellence?

A heuristic framework proposed by Voyant, Amaro e Silva, Fichaux, Menard, Cros and Mamouri (SSRN preprint, 2026, doi:10.2139/ssrn.6877118). It treats excellence as a multiplicative system: four foundations (Rigour, Transparency, Visibility, Adoption), where a weakness in one collapses overall impact, and three amplifiers (Collaboration, Outreach, Societal relevance). It is a reflection tool, not a scoring instrument, and claims no empirical validation.

How can researchers make their work more visible?

The talk argues that impact depends on whether work can be found, trusted and adopted, not only on its quality, and that low-effort discoverability practices compound over time: persistent identifiers, open code with a DOI, clear pages that state the result and how to cite it. Collaboration helps only when it strengthens these foundations.

Key ideas

  • Low-effort discoverability practices compound into substantial long-term impact.
  • Collaboration amplifies only when it strengthens foundations, not merely visibility.
  • AI governance infrastructure is moving from best practice to legal requirement.

In the press: op-ed in News Tank (French)

The same argument, written for French higher-education and research leaders: Repenser l’excellence scientifique à l’ère de la saturation : vers une ingénierie de l’impact ? [Rethinking research excellence in an age of saturation: towards impact engineering?], op-ed by Cyril Voyant, News Tank Éducation & Recherche, 3 March 2026 (subscriber access). It defines excellence as the probability that a work is found, judged credible and reused, and lists what institutions can do: minimum reproducibility requirements, professional management of metadata and researcher identifiers, recognition of non-text outputs such as code and data, training doctoral students in making work reusable, and avoiding simplistic indicators.
