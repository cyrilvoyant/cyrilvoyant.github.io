The guiding thread of all the work

Understand before you compute

I start with the problem to solve. If known physical or mathematical relationships are sufficient, I use them directly. Otherwise, I use data to supplement the model or to build a forecast. I keep a more complex method only when its gain is demonstrated. AI (artificial intelligence) is one possible tool; verification and responsibility remain human.

A simple picture sums this up: three nested circles. At the center, the people who ask the question and take the decision; around them, a model we understand; on the outside, the tools, AI included, called on only when they help.

  • Radiobiology: compute dose equivalence under the assumptions of the chosen model, then state its limits.
  • Solar forecasting: compare every model with persistence (repeating the last observed value), on the same data and at the same horizon (how far ahead the forecast looks).
  • AI: help people use a scientific code without confusing a plain-language answer with a verified result.

In brief

  • Start from the problem and the person who decides: the question and the responsibility stay human.
  • Compute directly when the link is known; when the model falls short, complete it with learning rather than replace it.
  • Otherwise, learn with restraint: each level of complexity must beat the previous one under the same protocol, starting with persistence.
  • AI, deep learning and language models included, is a tool at the periphery: a language model may prepare a calculation, never guess it.
  • The ladder below gives, for each step, when to use it, a published example and the reference to beat. A shorter version, for all readers: the approach on www.cyrilvoyant.com.

The complexity ladder, step by step

At the center, the people who ask the question and decide. At the core, a model we understand. At the periphery, AI, one tool among others.

Pick a step. At each one, the same question: is this level enough against its reference?

1 · Compute directly

When to use it
The link between inputs and outputs is known: a physical law, a published and validated model. When no measurements exist, simulate from what is known.
Example on this site
LQL-Equiv: radiotherapy dose equivalence, computed by an explicit model (SoftwareX, 2026). Also: Tunnel Load Simulator, the energy demand of a road tunnel simulated without measurements (SoftwareX, 2026).
The reference to beat
No forecast to beat: the calculation must reproduce an independent implementation. LQL-Equiv is checked against the 2014 MATLAB version on 4,438 treatment schedules.

Is this level enough?

2 · Hybridize

When to use it
An explicit model exists but does not explain everything: keep it, and let learning estimate only what is missing.
Example on this site
Radioiodine and thyroid cancer: the compartmental model of Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, calibrated on 50 patients (Scientific Reports, 2026), the base of a hybrid digital twin in progress.
The reference to beat
The model alone. Learning is added only where it does better than the model, on the same data.

Is this level enough?

3 · Learn frugally

When to use it
The useful relations are not known well enough, but data exist. Start with the lightest tool that does the job, such as an ELM (extreme learning machine: a small neural network whose output weights alone are fitted).
Example on this site
One ELM for the whole Corsican grid: a single model forecasts seven signals at once; one hour ahead, the normalized error (nRMSE, lower is better) is 0.179 for solar (Applied Energy, 2026).
The reference to beat
Persistence (repeat the last value), then the structured references BLEND and CLIPER and ARTU, then a statistical model. Without a clear-sky model, the ELM matches or outperforms an ARMA (autoregressive moving average) model and quantile regression (Solar Energy, 2025).

Is this level enough?

4 · AI in its place

When to use it
Deep networks, only if they beat frugal learning under the same protocol. Language models, to read a request written in plain language, never to compute.
Example on this site
WebSemantic: a language model maps the request to the inputs of a validated code, which computes every number. Rules for using AI in a lab: AI in research.
The reference to beat
The best frugal model of the previous step: on the satellite benchmark (open code), U-Net and LSTM, two deep networks, do not beat the ELM reading its own cell. For a language model, the reference is the validated code alone: the result must be the same.

Is this level enough?

5 · Demonstrate trust

When to use it
At every step, before any use: evaluate against an explicit reference, publish code, data and limits.
Example on this site
NICE: a forecast error divided by that of persistence (Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments, 2025); the γ-index, carried over from radiotherapy dose verification, judges a forecast with explicit tolerances (International Journal of Forecasting, 2026).
The reference to beat
Persistence. NICE = model error / persistence error: 1 means “no better than persistence”, below 1 means a gain. The RTVACOS framework covers the rest: work must be found, trusted and reused.

Numbers come from the papers cited; this page computes nothing.

The story, step by step

Read

One method, every field

Cyril Voyant works on very different questions: the dose of a radiotherapy treatment, the response of thyroid cancer to radioiodine, the production of a solar plant, the electricity mix of an island, the load of a road tunnel, a request written in plain language. The domain changes; the method does not.

1. Start with the direct approach

When the link between inputs and outputs is known, compute it. Radiobiological dose equivalence follows an explicit model (LQL-Equiv). A compartmental model describes radioiodine kinetics in thyroid cancer. When no measurements exist, a tunnel's load is simulated from what is known (Tunnel Load Simulator).

Before forecasting a signal, describe what in it can be predicted (sCV and F: how variable the sun is, and how much of that variability can be anticipated).

2. Hybrid: keep the model, learn only what is missing

Between the model alone and learning alone lies the route Cyril Voyant prefers: hybridization. When an explicit model meets the need, no learning is added without reason. When it falls short, it is completed, not replaced: learning, informed by physics, medicine or mathematics, estimates only what the model does not capture.

  • Thyroid: Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi are preparing a hybrid digital twin built on their compartmental model (the method).
  • Solar forecasting: cyclostationary references write symmetry constraints into the forecast (BLEND and CLIPER).
  • As early as 2012: a numerical weather model combined with ARMA (autoregressive moving average, a classic time-series model) and neural networks (Energy, 2012).

3. Climb the complexity ladder only when needed

When the useful relations are not known well enough, compare methods one step at a time. First a simple reference, persistence: repeat the last value. Then a structured reference: BLEND and CLIPER, weighted averages of persistence with cyclic persistence or with the mean, and ARTU, which weighs the last two values and the mean.

Next come a statistical model, a frugal learning machine (ELM, extreme learning machine: a small neural network with fixed random hidden weights), and only then deep networks or foundation models. Each step is kept only if it beats the previous one under the same protocol, which is what NICE (a forecast error divided by that of persistence) and the out-of-sample benchmarks measure.

  • Thyroid: the next step adds an ELM only where the compartmental model is not enough.
  • Satellite forecasting: a per-cell ELM beats the same ELM reading the whole field, with a thousand times fewer parameters, and the deep networks tested.
  • Corsican electricity mix: an ELM learns the links between sources that no equation gives.

4. Language models: a tool on the ladder, not its top

Language models are used and useful: they read a request written in plain language. They do not replace a validated model. In WebSemantic, a language model maps the request to the inputs of a validated code, and the code computes every number.

5. Trust is demonstrated

Whatever the field, the result is evaluated with metrics that match the question (NICE, the γ-index), published with its code, data and limits, and archived with a DOI (a permanent identifier). This is the point of the RTVACOS framework (rigor, transparency, visibility, adoption, collaboration, outreach, societal relevance): research must be found, trusted and reused.

The same questioning applies to research itself. With J. P. A. Ioannidis (Stanford University), J. Baas and R. Boverhof (Elsevier), institutions are mapped by impact next to research-integrity proxies (Royal Society Open Science, 2026). Influence is read against an explicit 2% baseline rather than by volume.

The thread. In medicine, energy and language alike: compute directly when the link is known; when the model falls short, hybridize; otherwise climb the ladder one step at a time, and only as far as it pays; keep people at the center.

The five principles

Principle 1

People at the center

A forecast or a calculation serves a human decision: a physician, a grid operator, an engineer. The question and the responsibility stay human.

In the work: Twenty years of clinical radiotherapy, where every computed dose concerned a patient; forecasts designed for the operators of island grids.

Principle 2

The model at the core

Understand first: physics, signal structure, statistics. Compute directly when the link between inputs and outputs is known. When an explicit model meets the need, no learning is added without reason; when it falls short, hybridize: keep the model and let learning estimate only what is missing. When the useful relations are not known well enough, evaluate what the data can teach.

In the work: Radiobiological dose equivalence computed directly (LQL-Equiv); radioiodine therapy of thyroid cancer described by a compartmental model before any learning (Fusella Giuntini et al., 2026); variability understood before forecasting (sCV and F); signal structure exploited (BLEND).

Principle 3

Complexity only if it pays

No a priori on the method: start simple, and climb only if, and only as far as, it is needed. Each step (persistence, structured reference, linear model, extreme learning machine, deep network) must beat the previous one under the same protocol. Simpler is often no worse, and sometimes better.

In the work: Errors measured against persistence (NICE); demanding simple references (BLEND, ARTU); a per-cell ELM beating the same ELM reading the whole field with a thousand times fewer parameters, and the U-Net and LSTM reading the field (satellite study); frugal benchmarks.

Principle 4

Every tool, at the periphery

AI belongs in every toolbox, not in every solution. Machine learning, deep learning and language models are valuable and used; they remain tools around an understood, validated core: a language model may prepare a calculation, never guess it. The preferred form is often hybrid: a model informed by physics, medicine or mathematics, with learning for what the model cannot explain.

In the work: Hybrid forecasting since 2012: a numerical weather model combined with ARMA and neural networks (Energy, 2012); multi-output machine learning where the links are unknown (MIMO-ELM), compared with LSTM, U-Net and foundation models; language models that map text to the inputs of validated codes (WebSemantic).

Principle 5

Trust is demonstrated

Evaluate with metrics that match the question; publish code, data and limits; make reuse possible.

In the work: The γ-index, carried over from radiotherapy dose verification; the public audit and correction of BLEND; open code with DOIs and Software Heritage archives; the RTVACOS framework on rigor, transparency, visibility and adoption.

Principle 2 · The model at the core

When the link is known, compute it directly

The direct approach is the first choice in every field, because it is exact, explainable and needs no training data.

  • LQL-Equiv: radiobiological dose equivalence (BED and EQD2, doses made comparable across fractionation schedules; NTCP and TCP, the probabilities of normal-tissue complication and of tumor control) computed from the linear-quadratic-linear model, checked against the 2014 MATLAB version on 4,438 schedules.
  • Radioiodine therapy in metastatic thyroid cancer: a compartmental model of activity decay, tumor growth and thyroglobulin kinetics (Scientific Reports, 2026).
  • Tunnel Load Simulator: no measurements exist, so the load is simulated from geometry, traffic, ventilation and lighting: one year at 10-minute resolution in about 150 ms, with P10–P90 envelopes (the band holding 80% of the simulated draws).
  • sCV and F: how variable and how forecastable a site is, described before any model is fitted.
Principle 4 · Every tool, at the periphery

When it is not, let the machine learn it, frugally

Machine learning is used where no equation links the inputs and the outputs, starting with the lightest learner that does the job.

  • MIMO-ELM (MIMO: multiple inputs, multiple outputs): a simple, parsimonious and frugal learner: one extreme learning machine forecasts seven grid signals of Corsica at once; one hour ahead, nRMSE 0.179 for solar and 0.051 for thermal, trained in closed form in seconds, ahead of an LSTM tuned with a realistic budget.
  • PV_ELM_pred: persistence, BLEND, autoregression and 14 ELM variants on the same splits, parameters counted next to the error.
  • Thyroid, next step: an ELM is added where the compartmental model is not enough (work in progress).
  • Dimensionality reduction: the input is compressed only when the compression serves the forecast.
Principle 2 · The model at the core

When the model falls short, hybridize

Keep what is understood, learn only what is missing: learning informed by physics, medicine or mathematics, rather than a black box in place of the model.

  • Thyroid cancer and radioiodine: a compartmental model by Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with C. Voyant and D. Taieb, calibrated on 50 patients (Scientific Reports, 2026); the hybrid digital twin that adds learning where the model falls short is in progress.
  • Symmetry-constrained forecasting: cyclostationary references whose weights follow the periodic statistics of the signal: mathematics written into the forecast (Applied Mathematical Modelling, 2026).
  • NWP and hybrid ARMA/ANN: a numerical weather prediction model combined with time-series models (ARMA) and artificial neural networks (ANN) to forecast global radiation (C. Voyant, M. Muselli et al., Energy 39, 2012), followed by a hybrid methodology for hourly forecasting (Renewable Energy 53, 2013).
Principle 3 · Complexity only if it pays

Climb only if it pays

No a priori on the method: start simple, and climb if, and only if, it is needed. Each step must beat the previous one under the same protocol; most of the time the climb stops early, and the simpler model is not only no worse, but sometimes better.

  • Clearsky-free forecasting: removing the clear-sky model makes the forecasting chain simpler, and the ELM matches or outperforms ARMA with McClear clear sky and quantile regression, deterministic and probabilistic (Solar Energy 294, 2025): simpler is not worse, and sometimes better.
  • How much space is enough: an ELM reading its own cell (nRMSE 0.308) beats the same ELM reading the whole field (0.355) with a thousand times fewer parameters, and also beats U-Net (0.343) and LSTM (0.326).
  • BLEND and CLIPER: persistence references built on the cyclostationary structure of the signal, hard to beat before any trained model.
  • ARTU and stochastic persistence: second-order and clear-sky references (ARTU weighs the last two values and the mean), computed by hand.
  • Deseasonalization: the seasonal structure is removed only if doing so improves the forecast.
Principle 5 · Trust is demonstrated

Measure what a model adds

A model is judged against a reference and with tolerances that match the use, never on a raw error alone.

  • NICE: the error divided by that of persistence: 1 means no better than persistence.
  • γ-index: a forecast judged within explicit tolerances in space, time and intensity, a method carried over from radiotherapy dose verification.
  • BLEND v1.1.1: a public audit and correction of the open code, with out-of-sample results.
Principle 4 · Every tool, at the periphery

Language models: a tool, not the solution

AI belongs in every toolbox, not in every solution.

  • WebSemantic: left alone, a language model fills gaps by guessing, which changed results by up to a factor of 2.6 in plausible interpretations; here it only maps words to the inputs of a validated code, which computes every number.
Principle 1 · People at the center

Trust is demonstrated, and reuse made possible

Code, data and limits are published, archived and citable, so that the work can be found, trusted and reused.

  • Research excellence: the RTVACOS framework (rigor, transparency, visibility, adoption) and the op-ed on research in an age of saturation.
  • Questioning research itself: with J. P. A. Ioannidis, J. Baas and R. Boverhof, a science-wide map of institutions that sets impact next to research-integrity proxies (Royal Society Open Science, 2026); an Influence Index that reads influence against a 2% baseline on a doubling scale (open code and data; application to French universities in submission).
  • Methods and code: open repositories with Zenodo DOIs and Software Heritage archives, one guide per method.

Short biography

Cyril Voyant is a physicist and Research Director at Mines Paris – PSL (O.I.E.). Combining medical physics in radiotherapy (since 2003) and energy-forecasting research (PhD in 2011), he works on solar and energy forecasting, forecast evaluation and frugal AI. His approach: understand the problem with a model first, add complexity only when it pays, and keep people, not tools, at the center.
