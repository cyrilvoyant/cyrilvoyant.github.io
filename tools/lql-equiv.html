LQL-Equiv: free online EQD2, BED, NTCP and TCP calculator for radiotherapy

Cyril Voyant · Updated 2026-10-18

Open the calculator
Free, no installation. Runs entirely in your browser: nothing you enter leaves the page.

Biologically equivalent doses in radiotherapy, under the linear-quadratic-linear model. LQL-Equiv computes the biologically effective dose (BED), the equivalent dose in a reference fractionation (EQD2 and others), the normal-tissue complication probability (NTCP), the tumour control probability (TCP) and the radiation-induced cancer risk. It handles successive treatment courses separated by gaps, two fractions a day, accelerated proliferation and overall treatment time.

For research and education only. Not a medical device. Outputs must not be used to choose or modify a prescription, to compensate a real interruption, to sum dose for a reirradiation decision, or to predict a probability for an individual patient. NTCP and TCP are model illustrations that have never been validated against clinical outcome.

Validated against the original, case by case

Version 3.0 is a complete reimplementation of the 2014 MATLAB application. The 2014 software was run over 4,438 treatment schedules: every organ/tumour pair, a wide fractionation grid, gaps, twice-daily schedules and random multi-course plans.
Values compared 69,131
Agreeing at the 2014 output precision 69,125 (99.991%)
Cases differing 2 of 4,438, both exact ties of the 2014 search grid

Sixteen analytical checks that do not rely on the 2014 code (continuity at the transition dose, EQD = n·d at the reference fraction size, additivity, round trip) test whether the calculation itself is right. One of them revealed the only change that moves a number: overall time is now counted once instead of twice. The 2014 behaviour stays available for recomputing older results.

What it covers

  • 34 organs at risk and 20 tumour sites, each parameter with its documented provenance.
  • Linear-quadratic model with a linear tail above the transition dose; Dale and Van Dyk proliferation; Thames incomplete-repair correction; Lyman NTCP; logistic or Poisson TCP; linear-exponential cancer risk.
  • Also a Python library with no dependency beyond the standard library: pip install -e ., then compute(...).

Typical uses in teaching and research

  • Show students how EQD2 depends on dose per fraction and on α/β.
  • Compare hypofractionated and conventional schedules on the same organ.
  • Explore how a treatment gap changes the equivalent dose under the model's assumptions.
