LQL-Equiv: free online EQD2, BED, NTCP and TCP calculator for radiotherapy

Principle 2 · The model at the coreThe approach

In the story: when the link is known, compute it directly. No data and no AI are needed: the linear-quadratic-linear model gives the equivalent dose, checked against the 2014 version on 4,438 schedules.

In brief

  • LQL-Equiv computes biologically effective doses (BED), equivalent doses (EQD2), NTCP and TCP under the linear-quadratic-linear model, for 34 organs at risk and 20 tumour sites, in the browser.

Biologically equivalent doses in radiotherapy, under the linear-quadratic-linear model. LQL-Equiv computes the biologically effective dose (BED), the equivalent dose in a reference fractionation (EQD2 and others), the normal-tissue complication probability (NTCP), the tumour control probability (TCP) and the radiation-induced cancer risk. It handles successive treatment courses separated by gaps, two fractions a day, accelerated proliferation and overall treatment time.

For research and education only. Not a medical device. Outputs must not be used to choose or modify a prescription, to compensate a real interruption, to sum dose for a reirradiation decision, or to predict a probability for an individual patient. NTCP and TCP are model illustrations that have never been validated against clinical outcome.

The theory

Direct calculation. The link between a fractionation schedule and its biological effect is given by an explicit model, so it is computed, not learned. No data and no AI are needed: this work stops at the first rung of the complexity ladder.

Definitions

\(n,\ d\)
number of fractions and dose per fraction (Gy)
\(\alpha/\beta\)
tissue parameter of the linear-quadratic model (Gy)
\(d_T\)
transition dose above which the response becomes linear (Gy)
\(\gamma/\alpha\)
slope of the linear tail (library value 5)
\(T,\ T_k,\ T_p\)
overall time, kick-off time and potential doubling time of the tumour (days)

Biologically effective dose

Linear-quadratic (LQ), for \(d\lt d_T\):

$$\mathrm{BED}=n\,d\left(1+\frac{d}{\alpha/\beta}\right).$$

Linear-quadratic-linear (LQL, Astrahan), for \(d\ge d_T\): the curve continues as a straight line, which avoids the unbounded growth of LQ at high dose per fraction:

$$\mathrm{BED}=n\left[d_T\left(1+\frac{d_T}{\alpha/\beta}\right)+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\,(d-d_T)\right].$$

Two fractions a day, 6 h apart: the quadratic term is multiplied by \(1+H_2\) (Thames incomplete repair), \(H_2=\frac{\phi}{1-\phi}\left(2-\frac{1-\phi^2}{1-\phi}\right)\), \(\phi=e^{-0.693\cdot6/T_{1/2}}\). Tumour repopulation removes \(\frac{\ln2}{\alpha T_p}\,(T-T_k)\) Gy when \(T\gt T_k\).

Equivalent dose

\(\mathrm{EQD}_{x}\) is the total dose in fractions of \(x\) Gy (2 Gy for EQD2) that gives the same BED; without time correction and for \(x\lt d_T\):

$$\mathrm{EQD}_x=\frac{\mathrm{BED}}{1+x/(\alpha/\beta)}.$$

With overall time, LQL-Equiv solves for the number of reference fractions numerically.

Model illustrations (never clinically validated)

$$\mathrm{NTCP}=\Phi\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{EQD}-TD_{50}}{m\,TD_{50}}\right)\quad\text{(Lyman, }\Phi\text{ the standard normal distribution)},$$ $$\mathrm{TCP}=\frac{1}{1+\left(TCD_{50}/\mathrm{EQD}\right)^{4\gamma_{50}}}\quad\text{(logistic)},$$

or, with the Poisson form,

$$\mathrm{TCP}=2^{-\exp\left(e\gamma_{50}\left(1-\mathrm{EQD}/TCD_{50}\right)\right)}.$$

Worked example: one fraction of 15 Gy to the rectum

Rectum: \(\alpha/\beta=3.9\) Gy, \(d_T=7.8\) Gy. LQ: \(15\,(1+15/3.9)=72.7\) Gy. LQL: \(7.8\,(1+7.8/3.9)+5\,(15-7.8)=23.4+36.0=59.4\) Gy, 18 % lower. The difference grows with the dose per fraction, which is why the choice of model matters when comparing hypofractionated and conventional schedules. The calculator then adds the time and repair corrections of the tissue.

Run it

The core package depends only on the Python standard library. Executed on 22 October 2026 (Python 3.12). Fictitious research case; not a medical device.

# pip install -e .   (from a clone of github.com/cyrilvoyant/LQL-Equiv-web)
from lqlequiv import Course, Prescription, compute, load_library

library = load_library()
plan = Prescription(courses=(Course(dose_per_fraction=2.0, n_fractions=39),), reference_dose=2.0)
result = compute(library.organ("Rectum"), library.tumour_site("Prostate"), plan)
print(f"BED {result.courses[0].bed_oar:.1f} Gy, EQD2 {result.eqd_oar_total:.1f} Gy, NTCP {result.ntcp_percent:.1f} %")

Output:

BED 102.1 Gy, EQD2 78.0 Gy, NTCP 43.4 %

Validated against the original, case by case

Version 3.0 is a complete reimplementation of the 2014 MATLAB application. The 2014 software was run over 4,438 treatment schedules: every organ/tumour pair, a wide fractionation grid, gaps, twice-daily schedules and random multi-course plans.
Values compared 69,131
Agreeing at the 2014 output precision 69,125 (99.991%)
Cases differing 2 of 4,438, both exact ties of the 2014 search grid

Sixteen analytical checks that do not rely on the 2014 code (continuity at the transition dose, EQD = n·d at the reference fraction size, additivity, round trip) test whether the calculation itself is right. One of them revealed the only change that moves a number: overall time is now counted once instead of twice. The 2014 behavior stays available for recomputing older results.

Questions about EQD2, BED and LQL-Equiv

How do I calculate EQD2 online?

Open LQL-Equiv, a free calculator that runs entirely in the browser (nothing entered leaves the page). Enter the dose per fraction, the number of fractions and the organ at risk or tumour site; it returns the BED, the EQD2 and, as model illustrations, NTCP and TCP. It is for research and education only, not a medical device.

What is the difference between BED and EQD2?

Under the linear-quadratic model, the biologically effective dose is BED = n·d·(1 + d/(α/β)) for n fractions of dose d. EQD2 is the total dose in 2 Gy fractions that gives the same BED: EQD2 = BED / (1 + 2/(α/β)). BED is an abstract quantity; EQD2 is expressed in clinically familiar 2 Gy-equivalent terms. LQL-Equiv adds a linear tail above a transition dose and can include overall treatment time.

Why use a linear-quadratic-linear (LQL) model?

The pure linear-quadratic model keeps curving upward at high dose per fraction. The LQL variant used in LQL-Equiv switches to a linear dose response above a transition dose, which matters when comparing hypofractionated and conventional schedules. The choice of model and parameters remains an assumption to state explicitly.

Can LQL-Equiv be used for treatment decisions?

No. It is for research and education only and is not a medical device. Its outputs must not be used to choose or modify a prescription, compensate a real interruption, sum dose for a reirradiation decision or predict a probability for an individual patient; NTCP and TCP have never been validated against clinical outcome.

What it covers

  • 34 organs at risk and 20 tumour sites, each parameter with its documented provenance.
  • Linear-quadratic model with a linear tail above the transition dose; Dale and Van Dyk proliferation; Thames incomplete-repair correction; Lyman NTCP; logistic or Poisson TCP; linear-exponential cancer risk.
  • Also a Python library with no dependency beyond the standard library: pip install -e ., then compute(...).

Typical uses in teaching and research

  • Show students how EQD2 depends on dose per fraction and on α/β.
  • Compare hypofractionated and conventional schedules on the same organ.
  • Explore how a treatment gap changes the equivalent dose under the model's assumptions.
