Tunnel Load Simulator: electricity demand of road tunnels, Monte Carlo web app

In brief

  • The Tunnel Load Simulator generates the electricity demand of a road tunnel from its geometry, traffic, lighting and ventilation, with random pollution episodes and accidents.
  • One year at 10-minute resolution runs in about 150 ms; Monte Carlo runs give medians and P10-P90 envelopes of energy, peak power and load factor.

How much electricity does a road tunnel use, and how uncertain is it? The Tunnel Load Simulator generates realistic time series of power and energy demand from the tunnel's geometry, operating context, traffic-driven ventilation, lighting regime and random disturbances such as pollution episodes and accidents. It runs N independent Monte Carlo realizations and returns median, P10 and P90 values of the key indicators, with an uncertainty envelope on the time series.

Fast: the engine is fully vectorized. One year at 10-minute resolution simulates in about 150 ms; 20 Monte Carlo runs in about 0.6 s.

The theory

Simulation from what is known. No measurements of the tunnel exist, so the load is built from what is known about its geometry, traffic, lighting and ventilation, with explicit random disturbances. No data are needed, and none are faked: the model is simple, its parameters are visible, and its limits are stated.

The load, term by term

At each 10-minute step \(t\), the power is the sum of four terms with a multiplicative noise:

$$P(t)=\max\!\Big(0,\ \big[P_0+P_{\mathrm{light}}(t)+P_{\mathrm{vent}}(t)+P_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)\big]\big(1+\varepsilon_t\big)\Big),$$ $$\varepsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).$$
Traffic \(q(t)\)
night floor \(0.16+0.06\cos\frac{2\pi(\tau-3)}{24}\) plus Gaussian morning and evening peaks, normalised by a one-day reference, then multiplied by context (urban 1.20, peri-urban 1.00, rural 0.78), season (winter 1.12 … summer 0.92), day of week (weekdays 1.00, Saturday 0.86, Sunday 0.78), traffic level and a daily random factor; clipped to \([0,2]\)
Lighting
\(P_{\mathrm{light}}=p_\ell\,L_{\mathrm{km}}\,n_{\mathrm{lanes}}\,\min\!\big(1,\ f_{\min}+(1-f_{\min})(1-D(t))+c_q\,q(t)\big)\), with \(D(t)\) a smooth daylight indicator from seasonal sunrise and sunset, and \((p_\ell,c_q,f_{\min})\) per technology (LED adaptive: 16 kW/km/lane, 0.18, 0.20)
Ventilation
\(P_{\mathrm{vent}}=p_v\,L_{\mathrm{km}}\,n_{\mathrm{tubes}}\,\big(0.15+s\,q(t)\big)\,a\,g\,\big(1+s_p\,\mathbb 1_{\mathrm{pollution}}(t)+s_a\,\mathbb 1_{\mathrm{accident}}(t)\big)\), with \(p_v\) per system (longitudinal: 120 kW/km/tube), altitude factor \(a=1+\max(z-500,0)/6000\) and slope factor \(g=1+|i|/20\)
Auxiliaries
\(P_{\mathrm{aux}}=p_a\,L_{\mathrm{km}}\,n_{\mathrm{tubes}}\,(1+\min(z_{\max},800)/6000)\,(1+\eta_t)\), \(\eta_t\sim\mathcal N(0,0.025^2)\)
Events
pollution episodes and accidents drawn each day with given probabilities, start times and durations

Monte Carlo and indicators

\(R\) independent realizations (seeds \(42,43,\dots\)) give distributions of every indicator; the envelope reports the median and the P10–P90 band. Annual energy \(E=\sum_t P(t)\,\Delta t\); load factor \(\bar P/P_{\max}\); specific consumption \(E/\text{length}\).

Assumptions and validity

A first-order synthetic generator: the coefficients are engineering orders of magnitude, not measurements. Suitable for prototyping, teaching and pre-design sensitivity studies; calibrate against measured data before any engineering use.

Run it

The public example: a 1.5 km peri-urban tunnel, two tubes of two lanes, adaptive LED lighting, longitudinal ventilation, one year at 10 minutes, 20 realizations. The package lives in src/, hence PYTHONPATH=src. Executed on 22 October 2026 (Python 3.12).

git clone https://github.com/cyrilvoyant/tunnel-load-simulator.git
cd tunnel-load-simulator
pip install -r requirements.txt
PYTHONPATH=src python examples/monte_carlo_example.py      # PowerShell: $env:PYTHONPATH="src"; python examples/monte_carlo_example.py

Output:

What you get

  • Annual energy (MWh), peak and mean power (kW), load factor, specific consumption (kWh/m/year) and event counts, for each run and as distributions.
  • Interactive charts and CSV export of the time series.
  • A Python package usable without the interface (run_monte_carlo(...)), with examples and tests.

Typical uses

  • Pre-design sensitivity studies: lighting technology, ventilation type, traffic level.
  • Synthetic load profiles for research on energy management and renewable integration in tunnels.
  • Teaching Monte Carlo uncertainty on an engineering case.

Status: first-order synthetic generator, suited to prototyping, demonstrations and pre-design sensitivity studies. Calibrate against measured data before any engineering or publication use.
