WebSemantic: scientific codes that ask before they compute

When a language model fills missing inputs by itself, plausible guesses changed computed results by up to a factor of 2.6 and moved a complication probability from 3% to 47%. WebSemantic checks every input against the code's contract and asks the user for what is missing; the scientific code itself stays unchanged.

Tunnel Load Simulator: electrical demand of road tunnels

Monte Carlo generator of tunnel electricity demand from geometry, traffic-driven ventilation, lighting regimes and random events, with P10–P90 envelopes and CSV export. One year at 10-minute resolution simulates in about 150 ms. SmartTunnel project with H. El-Houari (USMBA). First-order synthetic generator: calibrate on measured data before engineering use.

Research assessment and research excellence

Mapping institutions by their authors' impact and research-integrity proxies

With John P. A. Ioannidis (Stanford), Jeroen Baas and Roy Boverhof (Elsevier). Scopus-based, size- and subfield-adjusted institution-level data combining top-cited authors with three integrity proxies: retractions, extreme self-citation and publications in discontinued titles. Once results are expressed as proportions of top-cited authors rather than volume, the leading institutions change: mostly research institutes and tech organisations, only a minority of universities. Public datasets for exploration, not a single league table.

Practical method guides

Start here: solar forecasting, made simple — a guided route through the research, evidence and code.

Full English explainers: PV dimensionality reduction · Frugal solar forecasting.

Forecasting every source of an island grid at once: MIMO-ELM

Why forecast thermal, imports, hydropower, solar, wind and the total together; SISO, MIMO and MIMO-MH; a worked reconciliation example; EDF open data and open MATLAB/Python code. Based on the published Applied Energy article.

Radioiodine therapy in metastatic thyroid cancer: the RAIR-Sim model

Three equations for radioactive iodine activity, tumour cells and thyroglobulin, calibrated on 50 patients; an activity threshold that explains responding and refractory profiles; runnable Python and the original MATLAB. Work of Marie Fusella Giuntini and Dominique Barbolosi, with Cyril Voyant and David Taieb (Scientific Reports 2026).

How variable is the sun, and how forecastable? sCV and F explained

Separate variability from forecastability with sCV and F: definitions, a worked example, scientific figures and open MATLAB code. Based on the published Renewable Energy study; synthetic demonstrations are identified.

How much space is enough for satellite irradiance forecasting?

Compare one cell, a local patch, a whole field and compressed inputs on HelioClim-3 irradiance over Corsica. Open MATLAB/Python code, public benchmark results and explicit limits. Manuscript in preparation; data credited to SoDa.

Direct PV power forecasting: why reduce dimensionality?

Why forecast delivered PV power directly, and compress its history? Redundancy, geometric filters, forecast validation and a worked numerical example.

Solar forecasting: why deploy frugal models?

Why forecast close to the sensor? A practical explanation of model capacity, linear baselines, ELMs and deployment constraints, with numerical examples.

Does deseasonalization help solar and wind forecasting? The Make_Stationary benchmark

Frugal ELM deseasonalization tested on 9 solar, wind and load series: cleaner residuals do not mean better forecasts. Results, method, MATLAB code, citation.

How much model is enough for solar forecasting? The PV_ELM_pred benchmark

Open Python benchmark of persistence, linear AR and 14 Extreme Learning Machine variants for frugal PV, irradiance and weather forecasting at the edge.

Why compare a solar forecast with persistence? BLEND and cyclostationary persistence

The reference every forecast must beat, and closed-form alternatives with weights estimated on historical data: BLEND and CLIPER per phase or on the clear-sky index, with an audited open-code benchmark (v1.1.1); published study validated on 68 Spanish stations. Reference ladder, limits, MATLAB code, citation.

NICE metrics: evaluating forecasts against persistence

Definitions, interpretation and a numerical example for persistence-referenced deterministic forecast errors.

Gamma-index: evaluating displaced irradiance forecasts

Spatial, temporal and intensity tolerances for forecast verification, with explicit interpretation limits.

Research background

Cyril Voyant is a French physicist and Research Director at Mines Paris–PSL University, within the Centre Observation, Impacts, Énergie (O.I.E.). His main research area is solar irradiance forecasting, including global horizontal irradiance forecasting, photovoltaic power forecasting, time-series modelling, machine learning for energy systems, and operational forecast evaluation. He also has established work in medical physics, especially radiotherapy dosimetry and radiobiological dose-equivalence modelling.

Research focus

Solar irradiance and PV forecasting

Forecasting of solar radiation, global horizontal irradiance, photovoltaic production and renewable energy time series, with emphasis on short-term and multi-horizon operational use.

Time-series modelling and AI

Machine learning and statistical modelling for energy systems, including Extreme Learning Machines, autoregressive models, MIMO forecasting, cyclostationarity and benchmark design.

Forecast evaluation metrics

Development and use of rigorous deterministic forecast metrics, including NICEk, with attention to benchmark choice, statistical discrimination and operational interpretability.

Medical physics

Prior and continuing work on radiotherapy dosimetry, radiobiology, LQ/LQL dose-equivalence models and software tools for treatment interpretation and comparison.
